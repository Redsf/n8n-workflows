# LinkedIn Caption Rules — n8n Workflow Posts

A combined rulebook drawn from:
- High-performer analysis (Justin Welsh, Sahil Bloom, Lara Acosta style breakdowns)
- LinkedIn algorithm research (2023–2024 data)
- Copywriting frameworks used by top B2B content creators

---

## SECTION A: The Hook (First 3 Lines)

The LinkedIn "See More" button is the gatekeeper. If the first 3 lines don't stop the scroll, the algorithm stops showing the post.

**For n8n workflow posts, prefer these hook types (in order of effectiveness):**

### 1. The Contrarian Hook
Challenge a common belief held by Marketing & Growth people.
> "I stopped doing manual lead enrichment. Here's what replaced it."

### 2. The Specific Number Hook
Use real, precise numbers — not round ones. Specificity creates credibility.
> "This 7-node workflow saves our team 11 hours every single week."
> *(Not "saves hours every week" — that's vague and forgettable)*

### 3. The Negative Hook (Pain-first)
Open with the problem, not the solution. Pain drives more clicks than benefits.
> "Most growth teams are still copy-pasting data between tools. This is why they lose."

### 4. The If-Then Hook
Create a conditional that makes readers self-identify.
> "If you're still doing X manually, you need to see this."

### Hook Rules:
- **Never start with "I"** — LinkedIn's algorithm reportedly suppresses posts that open with "I" (observed pattern across high-reach accounts)
- **No more than 12 words** on the very first line — it must be fully visible before "See More"
- **No hashtags or emojis in the hook** — they break the pattern and signal low effort

---

## SECTION B: F-Pattern Formatting

People scan before they read. If the post looks like a wall of text, they scroll.

- **2 lines max** per paragraph — never more
- **"Breath" technique:** Every long sentence gets followed by a short, punchy one.
  - Long: "The workflow pulls lead data from LinkedIn, enriches it via Clay, and pushes it to HubSpot automatically."
  - Short: "Zero manual work."
- **Use custom bullets** for lists: ↳ → ✅ (avoid plain dashes — they look like a Word doc)
- **"1-3-1" sentence structure** for the body: One sentence opener → three lines of context → one punchline
- **Bold sparingly** — only for the single most important phrase in the post, if at all

---

## SECTION C: Establishing Deep Authority

For Marketing & Growth audiences, you must move from *"I think"* to *"I built/measured/observed."*

- **Kill weak language:** Delete "I think," "In my opinion," "Basically," "Just," "Maybe"
- **Avoid humble bragging:** Don't say "Excited to share this workflow!" — say what it does and what it produced
- **The Data + Story Combo:**
  - Pure data = boring
  - Pure story = anecdotal
  - Best format: *"Our lead response time dropped from 4 hours to 8 minutes (data). Here's the exact workflow that did it (story + value)."*
- **The Anti-Niche Take:** Occasionally reveal a hard truth about automation or marketing ops that other people avoid. This signals thought leadership.
- **For n8n posts specifically:** Name the tools in the stack. "n8n + Clay + OpenAI + HubSpot" is a credibility signal to the right audience.

## SECTION D: Copywriting Frameworks

Choose ONE framework per post. Don't mix them.

### PAS — Problem, Agitation, Solution
1. **Problem:** Name the specific pain ("Most teams spend 6+ hours a week on lead enrichment")
2. **Agitation:** Twist the knife ("That's 300 hours a year. 7.5 full work weeks. Gone.")
3. **Solution:** Present the workflow as the fix

*Best for:* posts where the workflow solves a clear, frustrating problem

### The Bridge
1. **Before:** Where the reader is now (pain state)
2. **After:** Where they want to be (outcome)
3. **Bridge:** The workflow is how you get there

*Best for:* transformational or "before vs after" stories

### The Listicle with a Twist
Give N tips/steps, but make the final one a mindset or philosophical insight that connects emotionally.

*Best for:* "Here's exactly how I built this" breakdown posts

### AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action
Classic but effective for workflow reveals.
- **Attention:** Hook (see Section A)
- **Interest:** What the workflow does and why it's clever
- **Desire:** The result / outcome it produces
- **Action:** The CTA

## SECTION E: The Call to Action (CTA)

"What do you think?" is not a CTA. It's lazy and it generates no engagement.

**Use only one of these:**

### Low Friction Question (Best for reach)
Give A or B choice. Requires zero thinking.
> "Do you build your automations in n8n (A) or Make (B)?"

### The Giveaway Strategy (Best for comments/algorithm boost)
Offer a resource in exchange for a comment keyword.
> "I've documented this exact workflow as a template. Comment 'WORKFLOW' and I'll DM it to you."
> *(This explodes comment count, which signals the algorithm to push reach)*

### The Tag Prompt (Best for impressions)
> "Tag one person on your team who still does this manually."

### The Curiosity Close
End with a question that teases your next post or next insight.
> "Next week I'll share the v2 that also handles the follow-up sequence. Want it?"

**CTA Rules:**
- Place the CTA as the very last thing in the post
- One CTA only — never stack two
- Keep it to 1–2 lines max

## SECTION F: Technical LinkedIn Algorithm Rules

- **The Golden Hour:** Engage with 10–15 posts *before* you publish your own. Signals active presence to the algorithm.
- **Stay in Comments:** Reply to every comment in the first 60 minutes. This creates engagement velocity that expands reach.
- **Dwell Time Matters:** Longer posts, carousels, and storytelling formats keep people on the post longer — which the algorithm rewards.
- **NO external links in the caption.** LinkedIn suppresses reach for posts with outbound links (YouTube, blog, etc.). Put any links in the **first comment**, or post first and edit the link in after 5 minutes.
- **Hashtags:** Use 3–5 max, placed at the very bottom. More than 5 looks spammy. Recommended for n8n posts: `#automation`, `#n8n`, `#growthhacking`, `#marketingops`, `#nocode`
- **Post timing (Marketing & Growth audience):** Tuesday–Thursday, 7–9am or 12–1pm in the audience's local timezone tend to outperform.

---

## SECTION G: Aesthetic & Professionalism Rules

- **Emoji restraint:** 1–3 emojis max, never in the hook. C-suite energy, not Instagram energy.
- **The Final Punchline:** End (before the CTA) with a one-sentence "mic drop" that distills the core lesson of the post.
  - Example: *"The best growth team isn't the biggest one. It's the most automated one."*
- **No clutter words:** I think / In my opinion / Basically / Just / Simply / Very
- **No corporate speak:** Leverage / Synergy / Circle back / Touch base — banned.
- **Ideal length:** 150–300 words for a high-performing LinkedIn post. Under 150 feels thin; over 400 loses people unless it's a structured listicle.

## SECTION H: n8n-Specific Captioning Tips

These are patterns that work specifically for workflow/automation content targeting Marketing & Growth professionals:

1. **Lead with the outcome, not the tool.** Don't open with "I built an n8n workflow." Open with "We cut our lead response time by 80%."
2. **Name the stack.** Listing the tools used (n8n → Clay → Slack → HubSpot) acts as a credibility marker AND helps with search/discoverability.
3. **The "What it replaced" line.** Always include one line about what manual process this automation killed. It makes the value tangible.
4. **The "Here's how it works" breakdown.** A simple ↳ step-by-step breakdown of the workflow nodes gets saved and reshared at high rates — people treat it as a reference.
5. **The "You can steal this" invitation.** Growth audiences respond strongly to posts that feel generous. Ending with "You can build this in under an hour" or offering the template as a giveaway drives massive engagement.

## Quick Pre-Publish Checklist

Before finalising any caption, verify:
- [ ] Hook stops scroll in under 2 seconds?
- [ ] No paragraph longer than 2 lines?
- [ ] Specific data point or outcome included?
- [ ] Weak/clutter words removed?
- [ ] Framework is clear and consistent throughout?
- [ ] Exactly one CTA at the end?
- [ ] No external links in the caption body?
- [ ] 3–5 hashtags at the bottom?
- [ ] Final punchline lands?
- [ ] Emoji count: 3 or fewer?
